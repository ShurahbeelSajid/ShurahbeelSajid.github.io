01 About

From mathematics to machine perception.

I am a researcher and AI engineer based in Islamabad, Pakistan. My training began in pure mathematics; my research now asks how models should represent the structure of interactions.

At Air University I completed an MS in Data Science with a thesis on unifying appearance and motion for human–object interaction recognition, supervised by Prof. Danish Hamid. That work became TransFlow-HOI, a cross-attention fusion model published in IEEE Access.

As a visiting research student at Northwestern Polytechnical University in Xi’an, on a full scholarship, I modelled Traditional Chinese Medicine prescriptions as hypergraphs, capturing multi-herb combinations that pairwise graphs cannot express.

Today I am a Jr. AI Engineer at CareCloud, building LLM and NLP pipelines for clinical documents and validating them against the judgement of clinical experts. Alongside that I build open-source systems such as CrisisWeave, an evidence-first retrieval assistant that abstains when the evidence runs out.

02 Research

How should interaction be represented?

Interactions are relational: a hand and a cup unfolding over time, several herbs combined in one prescription. Both of my research lines ask how that structure should be represented so that models learn relations rather than co-occurrence statistics.

Video understanding

Human–object interaction recognition

Appearance tells a model what is in the frame; motion tells it what is happening. My MS thesis fuses RGB appearance and optical-flow motion streams through cross-attention to model long-range temporal dependencies in interaction video.

MS thesis · Air University · Supervisor: Prof. Danish Hamid

  • HOI4D
  • I3D features
  • Optical flow
  • Cross-attention
Read the publication

Structured representation learning

Hypergraphs for multi-way relations

A prescription is not a list of herb pairs; it is one combination. I modelled Traditional Chinese Medicine prescriptions as herb–symptom networks and as hypergraphs, then used network-based pattern discovery to surface recurring co-prescription motifs and their symptom associations.

Bioinformatics R&D Lab · Northwestern Polytechnical University · 2024–25

Featured project Python

CrisisWeave

Evidence-first multimodal retrieval for disaster reports.

Retrieval-augmented question answering over heterogeneous disaster evidence: native text, OCR output, document images, video frames and transcripts, and structured tables. It validates citations, flags conflicting and stale sources, and abstains explicitly when the evidence is insufficient, preferring calibrated refusal to confident hallucination.

  • Guarded read-only SQL and approved-domain web search, under tenant-isolated evidence libraries
  • Configurable BGE embeddings and reranking, CLIP visual retrieval and Faster Whisper transcription
  • RAG
  • Qdrant
  • DuckDB
  • Tesseract OCR
  • FFmpeg
  • Docker

Illustrative query flow

“Which disaster type caused the highest direct economic loss, and on which page is it stated?”

  1. Retrieve matching passages and table rows
  2. Rerank and answer only from retrieved evidence
  3. Check every citation against its source page
  4. If support is missing, abstain instead of guessing
